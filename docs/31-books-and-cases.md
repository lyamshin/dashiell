# Books, and cases that aren't murder

*Proposal for the designer, 2026-09-24. Written alongside the sheets golden (`docs/golden/sheets-arrival.md`).*

## Books

> The designer: "We should also build 'books.' Books will be overall narrative arcs shaped by the crime or subject of investigation."

A **book** is the shape of a whole night: a sheet for the night, the way a page is made of sheets. Each case type has a few book variants. A book sets out:

1. **Acts, in order,** with the page sheets each act prefers:

   | act | what happens |
   |---|---|
   | the hook | the office and the client |
   | the scene | where it happened |
   | the widening | people, accounts, the neighbourhood |
   | the turn | the first lie caught, or a confession |
   | the narrowing | the half hour that matters |
   | the report and the ending | |

   The engine moves from one act to the next on what has happened, not on page count. For example, "the turn" starts when the first contradiction is held.
2. **Book roles that run across the whole night:**
   - **a motif** carried from the office to the ending, such as the client's gloves, the weather, or the detective's knuckle;
   - **a running gag** that pays off once in the ending, such as the radiator that finally works or the coffee that finally gets drunk;
   - **a question the client asked** that the ending answers in her terms.

   These are sheet roles at night scale, exported from the office page and redeemed later.
3. **Recaps at the act turns,** so the recap feels like a chapter break rather than an interruption.
4. **An ending sheet shaped to the case:** a murder ends with the report and the DA. A lost dog ends with the dog.

Books would come after page sheets work. They use the same machinery, one level up.

## Cases that aren't murder

> The designer: "Too much murder, too much money lending. Now that we're leaning camp we should have some more mundane relationships and requests. A lost item. A lost pet. Finding out about an affair. … I think that's part of what makes this feel like a slog."

The deduction machine is "where was everybody at the half hour that matters," and it doesn't need a body. It fits mundane cases as well as murder, arguably better.

| case | the half hour that matters | what the report asks | how it ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| **a lost pet** (a dog, a cat, a parrot) | when the gate or door was left open, or the pet was last seen | who let it out, and where it went | the pet, back, and a small scene about who let it out |
| **a lost item** (a ring, a watch, a set of teeth, a medal) | when it went missing from where it was kept | who had it last, and where it is now | the item, and whether anyone meant it |
| **an affair** (a spouse, a fiancé, a business partner) | the hour the client suspects | who was where, and with whom. The together/apart rules become the heart of it | what the detective tells the client, and what he doesn't |
| murder, robbery, missing person | as now | as now | as now |

The rules carry over:

- **The lie rule holds.** People lie about themselves. In an affair case, nearly everyone has a reason to.
- **Watchers stay honest.**
- **The grid, the confront verb and the report as a column** all work unchanged.

**Relationships beyond money.** Today a large share of ties are debts and loans. New ties:

- neighbours across a fence;
- an old flame;
- rivals in a bowling league or a chess club;
- the band;
- a landlady's feud over a cat;
- in-laws;
- a borrowed ladder never returned.

Money stays, but as one tie among many.

**Where the new cases go in the tiers.** Proposal: Raw and Coddled deal mostly mundane cases, a lost pet or a lost item, so the first runs are light, short and funny. Murder enters at Poached and becomes common from Soft-boiled. The affair case starts at Poached, where lies arrive, because it's all about who's lying about where they were.

## Questions for the designer

1. Are those the three new cases, or are there others you want: a forged letter, a stolen recipe, a missing band member before a gig?
2. Should the first tiers be mostly mundane, with murder saved for later?
3. For affairs, is the ending choice (what to tell the client) something the player decides? That would be a small, real decision at the end of a run.
4. Books after sheets, or do you want the book shape sketched as a golden now too?
