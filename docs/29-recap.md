# The recap

*Queued 2026-09-23. Build after M11 (people) lands; both touch the page planner.*

The designer: "we had discussed having the detective give a recap after something happened. I don't think that's there or isn't long enough." It isn't there. The closest thing is a one- or two-sentence thought after a find.

## What a recap is

A paragraph or two, in the detective's voice, taking stock of the case. It's how a person thinks after something has shifted. It says only what the notebook holds, and what's still open. It never concludes above Coddled. It's the detective laying the pieces out, not solving them.

It covers, in plain sentences:

- **when:** the time as the notebook has it, and any event that pins it;
- **where it happened, and how,** if known;
- **who has told their evening,** and whose account has another person's word under it;
- **who has been caught in a lie,** and what the lie covered;
- **who nobody has placed yet** at the half hour that matters;
- **what's still open,** and the one or two questions that would close the most. The questions are phrased as the detective's curiosity, never as a marked answer.

It ends on what the detective means to do next, in a sentence.

## When it comes

1. **After something happens.** It follows the page's own beats:
   - a lie is caught: a contradiction held against someone's own account;
   - a confrontation lands: a second story or a confession;
   - the time is narrowed;
   - a description is linked to a name.
2. **At the turn of every second hour,** if at least two new facts have come in since the last recap.
3. **On demand:** a free choice, **"Go over what I have"**. It costs no time and is available any time outside the office.

At most one recap per page. A recap never repeats one from earlier in the night: it covers what changed.

## Example

*Seed 3 at Medium, after the second fact is put to Hauck and she gives up her secret.*

> I sat back and went over it.
>
> Sirkin died in his walk-up between eight and half past, and the whistle off the river at half past eight was the nearest thing I had to a clock. Somebody got in with the only key or somebody let him drink with them. The coroner said chloral, so it was the drink.
>
> Hauck had told me the third floor, then the office, and now the truth: she'd been at the speakeasy selling things that weren't hers to sell. Hargrove could say whether she was, and I hadn't asked him yet. Crowninshield had her own evening and nobody else's word under it. Vitale's I had from Rafferty, and it had a hole in it at eight. Marchetti I had only seen at the bar. Nobody had told me where she was at half past eight, and she hadn't told me herself.
>
> That was where the night stood. I wanted Hargrove next, and then Marchetti's own account of her evening.

(Every clause is a notebook fact, or the absence of one: "nobody had told me where she was". There's no verdict: the paragraph never says Marchetti did it. It points at the gap, and the reasoning is left to the player.)

## Rules

- Say only what the notebook holds, plus the absences of facts. Absences may be named.
- No verdicts above Coddled. At Raw and Coddled, a recap may say who's cleared once two facts agree, as the thoughts do.
- Plain words. People by name the way the detective knows them, and strangers by description.
- The correspondence checker traces every clause, and the reader lint gets a no-verdict rule for recaps.
- Length: 80 to 180 words. It's long enough to feel like thinking, and short enough to keep the page moving.
- The design test must not move. The recap states nothing the player couldn't read off the grid. Test that a marks-follower who reads recaps does no better than today.
