# Golden example — seed 3, the opening

*Written by hand against the generated facts of seed 3 (Kreuzer, Sweeney, Grasso; the Lower East Side; a cold night; Dashiell behind on the rent with a split knuckle). This is the target the engine is measured against. It is not a template. It is what a good page reads like.*

*Stylist's brief: a page is one scene. The client talks in whole turns; Dashiell asks short questions. Description follows one thing at a time and stops. At most one figure per page, and it is never explained. Every paragraph picks up something from the paragraph before it. Facts arrive as speech or as things seen, never as a list.*

---

## Page one — the office

Midnight. Two rooms over a Chinese laundry on Rivington Street. The steam came up through the floor all day and quit at six, and by midnight the cold had the place back.

I was at the desk doing nothing about the rent when the stairs started. One person, taking them slow. A woman, by the heels.

She knocked once and came in without waiting. She was thirty or near it, in a good coat that had been somebody else's first, and she kept her left hand in the pocket. When she took it out to sit down I saw why. The little finger had been broken once and set by nobody.

"Mr. Dashiell."

"Sit down."

She sat. She did not take the coat off.

"My name is Gretchen Kreuzer. I write tickets at Feldman's, on Orchard Street. I know what a thing is worth." She said it as if it were a reference. "Martin Sweeney is dead. You know the name."

I knew it. Sweeney had the top floor of the residential hotel on Rivington and the liquor for four houses on the block, and the four houses stayed open because of it.

"I found him," she said. "Half past eleven, in his rooms. Somebody had hit him with something heavy, more than once. The precinct came and took my statement at the desk downstairs and went home."

"What were you doing in his rooms at half past eleven?"

"Collecting." She let that sit. "He owed me money. He still does. That is why I am here and not in bed."

"You want the man who did it."

"I want what I am owed. If the man who did it is where the money went, then yes. Start with Grasso. He works the pier when there is work. Sweeney broke him two years ago and he has said so in every bar on the street."

She put a roll on the desk with a rubber band around it. I did not count it. She watched me not count it.

"Twenty dollars a day," I said, "and I tell you what I find, not what you want."

"That will do."

## Page two — the suite

The suite was on the fourth floor at the back. The night man let me up without asking who I was, which told me the precinct had already been, and that somebody had paid him to stop caring.

The door was not locked. The room was as they had left it. A lamp lay on the rug with the shade off and the bulb whole. The bed had not been slept in. On the sill of the open window a cigarette had burned itself down to the paper and gone out in its own ash.

Sweeney had gone to the morgue, but the floor remembered where he had been.

I stood in the doorway and let the room tell me what it wanted to. It wanted to tell me about the window. Four floors up, and the latch thrown back.

The coroner's note was on the table under a glass, where the precinct had left it for whoever came next. Between half past nine and eleven. One blow to the back of the skull, perhaps more. He had not died at once.

At ten o'clock the El goes over the end of the block. For twenty seconds you cannot hear a thing under it. I wrote that down and went to find the night man again.

## What the golden does, as rules

1. **One scene, one line of attention.** The office page is about the woman. The suite page is about the room. Nothing else gets described.
2. **The client carries the exposition in speech.** Six of her turns hold every given: who died, where, when found, how it looked, what the precinct did, her tie, her purpose, her pointer. Dashiell's turns are one line each and do work: they force the next fact out.
3. **Description is sequential and stops.** Coat, hand, finger. Lamp, bed, sill. Each item is a plain sentence. Three items, then the page moves.
4. **Zero similes on page one. One figure on page two** ("the floor remembered"), unexplained.
5. **Cohesion by reference.** Every paragraph's first sentence contains a noun or pronoun from the paragraph before it (the stairs, she, the coat, the name, him, the roll). Nothing arrives from nowhere.
6. **Sentence length is short and plain, with one long sentence per page that carries the weight** (the four houses; the night man).
7. **Time is stated as a fact of the world, not a mechanic.** The El at ten is evidence, delivered as a thing everyone on the block knows.
8. **Dashiell has a body and a mood in one sentence each** (the rent, the cold), then gets out of the way.
